Actions of large holders (whales);

Changes in legislation;

Technical events on the Polygon blockchain;

The overall macroeconomic situation.

Complete price crashes to zero are possible, and you may lose all invested funds.

2.2. No Guarantee of Profitability 💸
PNJC is a utility token, not an investment instrument. The project does not guarantee:

Appreciation of the token's value;

Payment of dividends or interest;

Return of invested funds;

Price stability.

Any projections presented in marketing materials are mere assumptions and cannot be construed as guarantees.

2.3. Speculative Nature of the Market 🎢
The memecoin and broader crypto-asset market is subject to speculative bubbles. A significant portion of demand may be driven by hype rather than the token's actual utility. Should interest wane, the price may fall faster than you anticipate.

💧 3. LIQUIDITY RISKS
3.1. Insufficient Market Depth
PNJC is traded on decentralized exchanges (DEXs) — QuickSwap V2 (70% liquidity) and Uniswap V3 (30%). Liquidity depth may be limited, especially in the early stages. This may result in:

Inability to sell tokens at your desired price;

Significant slippage on large trades;

Sharp price declines when attempting to sell large volumes.

3.2. Dependence on Liquidity Providers
Liquidity on DEXs is provided by liquidity providers (LPs), who may withdraw their funds at any time. Although the project's LP tokens are locked for 12 months via the PNJCLiquidityLocker smart contract (address to be published post-deployment), external LPs have no such obligations. A reduction in liquidity may render trading impossible.

3.3. Impermanent Loss Risks ⚖️
If you are a liquidity provider in PNJC pools, you are exposed to impermanent loss, which arises from changes in the token's price relative to the other currency in the pool. This may lead to losses even if the PNJC price increases.

Technical failures (block stalls, delays);

Congestion (high gas fees);

Forks or protocol changes.

Setting gas parameters (gas limit, gas price);

4.4. Dependence on External Services 🌐

🏛️ 7. RISKS RELATED TO DECENTRALISED GOVERNANCE (DAO)
7.1. Vote Concentration
Ecosystem governance is carried out via DAO, where voting power is determined by the number of PNJC tokens (1 PNJC = 1 vote). In theory, large holders (whales) may collude to pass decisions that benefit only them, to the detriment of smaller holders.

7.2. Low Voter Turnout
Decision‑making requires a quorum (5% of circulating supply for standard proposals; 10% for charter amendments; 3% for emergency proposals). If turnout is low, critical decisions may not be adopted, slowing project development.

7.3. Malicious Proposal Risk
Any holder can submit a DAO proposal. There is a risk that malicious actors may push proposals aimed at stealing treasury funds or altering parameters in their favour. Although a multisig veto (3/5) is in place, it is used only in emergency cases and must be publicly justified.

7.4. Conflict of Interest
Multisig members (3 of 5) may act contrary to community interests if their keys are compromised or they act in bad faith. The key replacement procedure is complex and may take time.

🔥 9. RISKS RELATED TO THE DEFLATIONARY MECHANISM (GOLDEN PHOENIX)
9.1. Unpredictability of Burning
The Golden Phoenix Protocol automatically burns tokens, but the burn volume depends on external USDT inflows (revenues from partners, NFT marketplace, voluntary contributions). If inflows cease, the deflationary effect may diminish, affecting price dynamics.

9.2. Technical Failures
The protocol relies on the QuickSwap V2 Router and correct swap processing. If the router is updated or changed, the protocol may cease to function until the DAO adapts the parameters.

9.3. Parameter Manipulation Risk
Protocol parameters (burn percentage burnPercent, insurance fund share insurancePercent, buyback share buybackPercent, price floor priceFloor) are adjustable by the DAO. If a vote is captured, malicious actors could alter parameters to harm holders (e.g., reduce burning or increase the insurance share at the expense of rewards).

9.4. Risks Related to the buybackPercent Parameter ⚙️
The protocol uses the buybackPercent parameter (default 25%), which determines the share of funds (after reserving the insurance fund) allocated to token buybacks. The remaining funds are accumulated on the contract balance. This creates the following risks:

Reduced deflationary effect: If buybackPercent is set too low, the burned token volume will be insufficient to create significant deflationary pressure.

Buyback delay: Funds not directed to buybacks are accumulated on the contract balance, which may delay token burning.

Slippage risk: A high buybackPercent value may lead to significant slippage when converting USDT to PNJC, reducing buyback efficiency.

DAO dependency: The parameter is adjusted through DAO voting, which may introduce delays or inefficient management.

9.5. Risks Related to the Insurance Fund 🛡️
The protocol reserves 5% of incoming USDT in the insurance fund to protect the price when it falls below the established floor (price floor). However:

Fund assets may be used inefficiently or manipulated through DAO votes.

The fund does not guarantee full protection against declines — it only mitigates short‑term fluctuations.

In a prolonged bear market, the fund's assets may be insufficient to stabilise the price.

Using the insurance fund requires multisig approval (4 of 5 signatures), which may cause delays.

9.6. Smart Contract Technical Constraints ⚙️
The Golden Phoenix Protocol smart contract contains the following technical constraints that may affect its operation:

insurancePercent + buybackPercent == 30: The sum of these two parameters must always equal 30. This limits configuration flexibility.

burnPercent + rewardPercent == 100: The sum must always equal 100, ensuring full distribution of purchased tokens.

Parameter changes require DAO voting and may take up to 5 days.
