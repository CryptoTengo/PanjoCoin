## 1. Overview

This document defines the **partnership framework** for the PanjoCoin (PNJC) ecosystem.

Its purpose is to establish a structured approach for identifying, evaluating, developing, and maintaining strategic partnerships that support the long-term growth of the ecosystem.

Partnerships are intended to strengthen technology, education, community engagement, charitable initiatives, and social impact while supporting responsible and sustainable development.

### 1.1. Context

PanjoCoin is a Web3 ecosystem combining blockchain technology, SocialFi, GameFi, charitable initiatives, and transparent governance. The project is built on **Polygon PoS** with a fixed-supply, ownerless utility token.

## 5. Partnership Lifecycle

Every significant partnership should follow:

| Phase | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Identification** | Identify potential partners aligned with PNJC's mission. |
| **2. Evaluation** | Assess strategic alignment, capabilities, and reputation. |
| **3. Due Diligence** | Verify legitimacy, compliance, and compatibility. |
| **4. Approval** | Obtain governance approval (multisig/DAO). |
| **5. Agreement** | Formalise partnership terms (MOU, contract). |
| **6. Implementation** | Execute partnership activities. |
| **7. Performance Review** | Evaluate partnership effectiveness. |
| **8. Renewal or Completion** | Continue, expand, or conclude the partnership. |

---

## 6. Partner Evaluation

Potential partners may be evaluated based on:

| Criteria | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Strategic Alignment** | Does the partnership support PNJC's mission and goals? |
| **Technical Capabilities** | Does the partner have the required technical expertise? |
| **Reputation** | What is the partner's public reputation and track record? |
| **Operational Capacity** | Can the partner deliver on its commitments? |
| **Compliance Practices** | Does the partner comply with applicable laws? |
| **Long-Term Sustainability** | Is the partner committed to long-term collaboration? |
| **Community Value** | Will the partnership benefit the PNJC community? |

Evaluation criteria should be documented where appropriate.

## 7. Due Diligence

Before entering into significant partnerships, reasonable due diligence may include:

| Activity | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Organisational Review** | Assess the partner's structure and legitimacy. |
| **Public Reputation Assessment** | Review public information and track record. |
| **Regulatory Considerations** | Evaluate compliance with laws and regulations. |
| **Technical Capability Review** | Assess technical expertise and infrastructure. |
| **Security Assessment** | Review security practices (where applicable). |
| **Conflict of Interest Evaluation** | Identify any potential conflicts. |

The scope of due diligence should be proportionate to the nature of the partnership.

## 13. Data Protection

Where collaboration involves data processing, partners should:

- Comply with applicable privacy laws (GDPR, Georgian legislation);
- Protect confidential information;
- Limit access to authorised personnel;
- Use secure communication methods.

Data sharing should be governed by appropriate agreements where required.

## 14. Intellectual Property

Ownership of intellectual property should be clearly defined before collaborative work begins.

| Risk | Description | Mitigation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Strategic Misalignment** | Partner goals diverge from PNJC's mission. | Clear agreements, regular reviews. |
| **Reputational Impact** | Negative association with a partner. | Due diligence, ethical standards. |
| **Technical Integration Issues** | Challenges in integration. | Technical assessment, testing. |
| **Regulatory Developments** | Changes in laws affecting partnerships. | Compliance monitoring, legal review. |
| **Operational Challenges** | Execution failures. | Clear responsibilities, governance. |
| **Communication Failures** | Misunderstandings or delays. | Defined communication procedures. |

Mitigation measures may include:
- Due diligence;
- Clear agreements;
- Regular reviews;
- Defined governance;
- Risk monitoring.

## 19. Termination

A partnership may conclude due to:

| Reason | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Completion of Objectives** | Partnership goals have been achieved. |
| **Mutual Agreement** | Both parties agree to conclude. |
| **Strategic Changes** | Partnership no longer aligns with strategic priorities. |
| **Legal or Regulatory Considerations** | Compliance issues or regulatory changes. |
| **Material Breach of Agreement** | Significant violation of partnership terms. |

Termination should be managed professionally and in accordance with applicable agreements.
