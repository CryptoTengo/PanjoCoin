This Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Policy (hereinafter referred to as the "AML Policy" or the "Policy") is an integral part of the PanjoCoin (PNJC) Whitepaper v1.1, Tokenomics v1.1, Legal Disclaimer v1.1, Terms of Use v1.0, Risk Disclosure v1.1, Restricted Countries List v1.1, and Privacy Policy v1.1.
This document establishes the fundamental principles and measures aimed at preventing the use of the PNJC token (hereinafter referred to as the "Token") and related services for unlawful activities, including money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud, and other criminal acts.
IMPORTANT: This Policy is a simplified version intended for use during the initial stages of the Project. As the Project develops and expands to centralized exchanges, this Policy will be supplemented and enhanced in accordance with the requirements of specific jurisdictions and regulators.
________________________________________
1. GENERAL PROVISIONS
1.1. Purpose of the Policy
The primary objectives of this Policy are:
•	Preventing the use of the Token and Services for money laundering and terrorist financing;
•	Complying with applicable international and national AML/CFT standards;
•	Protecting the reputation of the Project and its users;
•	Promoting transparency and legality of Token transactions.
1.2. Applicable Legislation
This Policy has been developed in accordance with:
•	The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) Recommendations;
•	The legislation of Switzerland (as the jurisdiction governing this document);
•	The European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA), where applicable;
•	Other international standards and norms in the field of AML/CFT.
1.3. Policy Principles
The PanjoCoin Project adheres to the following AML/CFT principles:
•	Transparency — All Token transactions are recorded on the public blockchain and are verifiable.
•	Accountability — Each user bears personal responsibility for the legality of their actions.
•	Risk-Based Approach — AML/CFT measures are applied proportionally to identified risks.
•	Legal Compliance — The Project endeavors to comply with applicable laws in all jurisdictions where it operates.
________________________________________
2. RISK ASSESSMENT
2.1. Nature of the Token and Associated Risks
PNJC is a utility token deployed on the public Polygon PoS blockchain. Accordingly, there are certain risks associated with the potential use of the Token for unlawful purposes:
•	Blockchain Anonymity — Although the blockchain is public, token holders may remain pseudonymous, complicating the identification of ultimate beneficiaries.
•	International Nature — The Token is accessible to users worldwide, creating challenges in complying with the laws of all jurisdictions.
•	Transaction Speed — Token transactions can be executed instantaneously and without intermediaries, which may be exploited for rapid movement of funds.
2.2. Risk Mitigation
The Project implements the following measures to mitigate identified risks:
•	Publicity of All Transactions — All Token operations are recorded on the public blockchain, ensuring full transparency and verifiability.
•	Prohibition on Restricted Countries — Prohibition on holding and trading the Token for citizens and residents of countries included in the Restricted Countries List.
•	Public Distribution Wallets — All addresses associated with Token distribution are published and verifiable.
•	Multisig Treasury Control — Key wallets are managed through a 3-of-5 multisig, reducing the risk of unauthorized transactions.
•	Liquidity Lock — 50% of all tokens will be locked through an immutable contract for 12 months (code ready; deployment scheduled; exact date to be announced at deployment), preventing manipulation and ensuring long-term stability.

3. PROHIBITED ACTIVITIES
Users are strictly prohibited from:
•	Money Laundering — Using the Token to legitimize criminally obtained income.
•	Terrorist Financing — Using the Token to finance terrorist activities, organizations, or individuals.
•	Fraud — Using the Token in any fraudulent schemes, including phishing, pyramid schemes, Ponzi schemes, and others.
•	Illegal Operations — Using the Token for operations prohibited by the laws of the user's country.
•	Sanctions Evasion — Using the Token to circumvent international or national sanctions.
•	Drug, Weapons, and Other Prohibited Goods Trafficking — Using the Token to pay for illegal goods or services.

4. AML/CFT MEASURES
4.1. Application of KYC/KYB (When Required)
Currently, the Project operates exclusively on a decentralized platform (Uniswap V3 DEX), allowing users to interact with the Token directly through their wallets without providing personal information.
However, should the Project be listed on centralized exchanges (CEX) in the future, the Project will comply with their KYC (Know Your Customer) and AML procedures, including:
•	Collection of identification documents (passport, national ID);
•	Proof of address verification (utility bills, bank statements);
•	Sanctions list screening;
•	Source of funds verification (as required by the exchange).
Within its own ecosystem, the Project does not conduct mandatory KYC verification because:
•	The Token is decentralized and available on DEX;
•	All transactions are public and verifiable through PolygonScan;
•	The Project is not a financial intermediary and does not conduct operations on behalf of users.
4.2. Transaction Monitoring
The Project utilizes the public Polygon PoS blockchain, which ensures:
•	Transparency of All Transactions — Any Token operation is recorded on the blockchain and viewable.
•	Continuous Monitoring — The Project uses blockchain explorers (PolygonScan) to track large and suspicious transactions.
•	Chain Analysis — If necessary, transaction chain analysis may be conducted to identify links to suspicious addresses.
4.3. Blocking Suspicious Addresses
In the event that addresses associated with unlawful activities are identified (included in sanctions lists, blacklists, FATF lists, etc.), the Project:
•	Reserves the right to publicly notify the community of such addresses (without disclosing personal information);
•	Recommends that users not interact with such addresses;
•	Bears no responsibility for transactions conducted from such addresses.
Important: Given that the Token smart contract lacks freeze or blacklist functions, the Project cannot technically block transactions from specific addresses. This is consistent with the decentralization principles and absence of administrative privileges embedded in the Token's architecture (as stated in WhitePaper v1.1, Sections 1 and 4.1).
4.4. Refusal of Service
The Project reserves the right to:
•	Restrict or terminate access to the Website and Services for users from countries included in the Restricted Countries List and Absolute Prohibition Countries;
•	Refuse to provide information or other services to persons suspected of involvement in unlawful activities;
•	Block IP addresses from prohibited countries.

11. APPENDIX: INDICATORS OF SUSPICIOUS TRANSACTIONS
For reference, below are examples of indicators that may signal suspicious activity:
#	Indicator	Description
1	Structuring	Splitting large amounts into numerous small transactions to avoid monitoring thresholds
2	Unusual Activity	Sudden increase in transaction volume without apparent reason
3	Interaction with Restricted Addresses	Transactions with addresses included in sanctions or blacklists
4	Geographic Inconsistencies	Transactions from/to high-risk countries (included in FATF lists)
5	Absence of Economic Logic	Transactions lacking reasonable economic justification
6	Rapid Address Changes	Frequent wallet changes to conceal fund origins
7	Use of Mixers	Transactions through mixing services to conceal transaction chains
If you witness any of the above transactions or other suspicious activities, please report them to: aml@cryptotengo.com.
