1. Introduction

PanjoCoin (PNJC) is a community-driven ERC-20 utility token operating on the Polygon blockchain.

This Anti-Money Laundering Policy ("AML Policy") establishes the principles, procedures, and compliance framework designed to prevent misuse of the PNJC ecosystem for:

Money laundering;
Terrorist financing;
Sanctions evasion;
Fraudulent activities;
Illegal financial operations.

PNJC is committed to maintaining transparency, responsible blockchain development, and alignment with applicable legal and regulatory principles.

This policy follows international AML/CFT principles, including risk-based approaches recommended for virtual assets and virtual asset service providers.

2. Regulatory Framework

PNJC operates with consideration of:

Primary Jurisdiction

Republic of Georgia

Applicable Georgian legislation related to:

Virtual assets;
Financial crime prevention;
Anti-money laundering requirements;
Consumer protection;
Data protection.
International Standards

PNJC considers:

FATF Recommendations;
FATF Virtual Asset and VASP guidance;
Sanctions compliance principles;
International AML/CFT best practices.

FATF guidance emphasizes that virtual asset activities should be evaluated using a risk-based approach and appropriate measures should be applied depending on risk exposure.

3. Scope of Application

This AML Policy applies to:

PNJC project operations;
Treasury management;
Charity wallet operations;
Community reward programs;
Partnerships;
Future ecosystem services.

This policy does not create obligations for independent users conducting ordinary blockchain transactions through decentralized exchanges.

4. Nature of PNJC Activities

PanjoCoin currently operates as:

A decentralized ERC-20 token;
A community ecosystem asset;
A utility token supporting future ecosystem applications;
A social impact initiative connected with charity activities.

PNJC does NOT currently:

Hold customer funds;
Provide custodial wallet services;
Operate fiat exchange services;
Execute financial transactions on behalf of users;
Manage user private keys.
5. AML Principles

PNJC follows five core AML principles:

5.1 Transparency

The project maintains transparency regarding:

Token supply;
Token distribution;
Treasury wallets;
Liquidity allocation;
Smart contract information.
5.2 Risk-Based Approach

AML controls are applied according to identified risks:

Low Risk:

Normal token holders;
Community participants;
Public blockchain users.

Medium Risk:

Large token transfers;
Partnership wallets;
Treasury interactions.

High Risk:

Suspicious wallet activity;
Sanction-related exposure;
Known illicit blockchain addresses.

7. Blockchain Monitoring

PNJC may use blockchain analytics tools to monitor:

Suspicious transaction patterns;
Known malicious addresses;
Sanctions-related addresses;
Fraud indicators.

Monitoring objectives:

Protect ecosystem integrity;
Reduce misuse risks;
Support responsible blockchain participation.
8. Sanctions Compliance

PNJC does not intentionally support:

Sanctioned entities;
Terrorist organizations;
Criminal organizations;
Illegal financial activity.

The project reserves the right to restrict partnerships, integrations, or ecosystem participation where significant compliance concerns exist.

9. Treasury AML Controls

Treasury operations should follow:

Authorization Controls

Future treasury management should use:

Multisignature wallets;
Defined approval procedures;
Transaction records.

Recommended structure:

Minimum 3-of-5 multisignature governance.

10. Charity Compliance

Charity allocations must maintain:

Transparency;
Donation records;
Public reporting;
Recipient verification.

Charity activities should follow applicable Georgian and international requirements.

11. Exchange and Partnership Due Diligence

Before cooperation with:

Exchanges;
Payment providers;
Web3 platforms;
Institutional partners;

PNJC may perform:

Reputation checks;
Compliance review;
Risk assessment;
Contract review.
12. Prohibited Activities

PNJC ecosystem must not be used for:

Money laundering;
Fraud;
Terrorist financing;
Sanctions avoidance;
Illegal fundraising;
Market manipulation.
13. Reporting Suspicious Activity

Users and partners may report suspicious activity through official PNJC communication channels.

Reports should include:

Wallet address;
Transaction hash;
Description of concern;
Supporting information.
14. Data Protection

Any collected compliance information must be handled according to:

Georgian data protection requirements;
Applicable international privacy principles.

PNJC minimizes collection of personal information unless legally required.

15. VASP Consideration

PNJC acknowledges that future ecosystem development may require regulatory assessment.

If PNJC activities expand into regulated virtual asset services, the project will evaluate:

VASP registration/licensing requirements;
Additional AML/KYC procedures;
Regulatory obligations.
16. Policy Updates

This AML Policy may be updated due to:

Regulatory changes;
Ecosystem expansion;
Security improvements;
International compliance developments.
17. Disclaimer

This document represents the AML compliance framework of PanjoCoin.

It does not constitute legal advice.

Users should comply with applicable laws and regulations in their own jurisdictions.
